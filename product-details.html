The Architecture of a Masterpiece

An heirloom saree is not manufactured; it is composed thread-by-thread over weeks of disciplined devotion.

Woven across four dedicated weeks in the heritage weaver enclave of Madanpura, Varanasi, the Meher saree represents the pinnacle of the ancient Kadhwa weaving technique. Unlike contemporary jacquard fabrics where loose threads remain floated on the reverse, in genuine Kadhwa each floral leaf and shikargah motif is engraved independently by the weaver using a miniature hand-shuttle.

"When you touch the reverse side of a true Kadhwa saree, your fingernails will never snag. It is as smooth as fine calligraphy on vellum."
Master Craftsman Mohammed Rafiq, 3rd Generation Varanasi Pitloom Weaver

The border features an architectural temple cornice with micro peacock (mayil) medallions, while the pallu culminates in a dense river of pure silver-gold zari brocade.

Cleaning Protocol
  • • Professional Dry Clean Only: Never immerse pure katan silk or tested zari in water or machine washes.
  • • In case of accidental liquid spills, dab gently with an unbleached cotton swab; never rub aggressively.
  • • Iron solely on the reverse side on low-heat silk setting, preferably under a thin muslin cloth.
Storage in Archival Muslin
  • • Always store wrapped in the complimentary pure unbleached cotton muslin bag provided by Katha Bbeen.
  • • Avoid plastic or synthetic bags, which trap ambient humidity and can oxidize fine zari over decades.
  • • Unfold and gently change the creasing lines every 6 months to preserve the resilience of the silk filaments.
